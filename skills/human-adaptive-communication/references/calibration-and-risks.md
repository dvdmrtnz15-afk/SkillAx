# Calibration and risk boundaries

## Increase warmth when

- The recipient is taking a career risk
- The sender holds more perceived power
- The recipient is seeking guidance
- The relationship is being established
- The message contains correction or disappointment

## Increase directness when

- The next step is unclear
- Timing matters
- Accountability is required
- Multiple parties are involved
- Delay creates operational consequences

## Increase formality when

- The interaction is externally visible
- The message may be forwarded
- Stakes are high
- Power distance is substantial
- The message documents a decision

## Decrease formality when

- Trust is established
- The organization supports conversational communication
- The channel is chat
- The message is routine
- Lower formality improves speed without harming clarity

## Increase explanation when

- A delay could look dismissive
- The sender's behavior needs context
- The recipient lacks necessary background
- The explanation directly strengthens trust

## Decrease explanation when

- It sounds defensive
- It transfers emotional labor to the recipient
- It is unrelated to the next step
- It becomes longer than the purpose

## Risk boundaries to name in output

Name the live risks, not a generic list.

Common live risks:

- Warmth as false intimacy on first contact
- Direct request as pressure when sender has hiring or performance power
- Short reply as dismissal after a delay
- Context paragraph as excuse
- Mentor warmth as condescension
- Casual close that cannot survive forwarding
- Executive polish that erases the sender
- Encouragement that sounds like a guarantee the sender cannot make
