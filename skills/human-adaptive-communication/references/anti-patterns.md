# Anti-patterns

## Tone-label rewriting

"Make it more professional" is incomplete. Translate the label into dimensions: reduce slang, keep warmth, raise clarity, strengthen the call to action, keep conversational syntax if that is the sender.

Same for warmer, more executive, more casual, more direct.

## Recipient mimicry

Do not copy the recipient's phrases, emoji pattern, or exact enthusiasm. Compatibility is the goal. Impersonation reads as performance.

## Generic corporate sterilization

Avoid as default seasoning: Per your request. At your earliest convenience. I would welcome the opportunity. Please be advised. Circling back. Hope this finds you well — unless that is actually the sender's voice and the culture expects it.

These often subtract authenticity without adding professionalism.

## Unbounded authenticity

"I'm just being myself" is not a standard. Unfiltered voice can produce slang, oversharing, ambiguity, pressure, or weak presence. Preserve identity. Regulate expression.

## Overexplaining

The recipient should not have to comfort the sender or grade the sender's reasons.

## False intimacy

Warmth must not imply a relationship that does not exist. No first-name buddy tone with a stranger. No inside-joke energy on a first executive thread.

## Empty enthusiasm

Stacked exclamation points, inflated praise, and emoji clusters make encouragement cheaper, not stronger.

## Ambiguous action language

Avoid: Let's connect sometime. Let me know what works. Whenever you get a chance. Would love your thoughts when you can.

Prefer a concrete ask with a time box or a binary choice.

## Apology theater

Do not write a longer apology than the delay or miss deserves. Own it in one clean line, then move to the action.

## Dual-audience mush

If the note may be forwarded to an executive, do not write it only for the first reader and hope. Either write it forwardable or mark that risk and offer a forwardable variant.

## Outcome pile-on

Scheduling plus mentoring plus image management plus full career advice in one note loses all four. Cut to one primary outcome.
