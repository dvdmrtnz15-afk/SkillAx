# Inference sequence and message architecture

## Sequence

### 1. Communication event

What happened, why a message is needed, what the sender is responding to, what decision or action follows.

### 2. Relationship map

Proximity, direction of influence, formal authority, informal authority, existing trust.

### 3. Stakes

Career significance, reputational risk, emotional sensitivity, forwardability, cost of misunderstanding.

### 4. Cues

Formality, warmth, directness, energy, length, structure, emotional openness. Cite the lines that produced the read.

### 5. Intended recipient state

Choose the few that matter: clear, respected, encouraged, accountable, reassured, motivated, comfortable responding, aware of urgency.

### 6. Intended recipient action

One primary action: send two times, confirm an assignment, provide information, change a behavior, attend a meeting, make a decision.

### 7. Tone profile

Set independent dimensions. Name the target in dimensional language, for example: warm, professionally conversational, mentor-like, moderately direct, low corporate stiffness.

### 8. Draft for function

Human acknowledgment. Purpose or orientation. Only necessary context. Specific request. Relationship-preserving close.

### 9. Misinterpretation test

- Could warmth read as overfamiliarity?
- Could directness read as pressure?
- Could brevity read as dismissal?
- Could context read as excuse-making?
- Could enthusiasm read as insincerity?
- Could authority read as condescension?
- Could informality weaken credibility?

### 10. Authenticity test

- Does this sound like the sender?
- Would the sender say this aloud?
- Did editing remove all personality?
- Did personality override situational judgment?

## Architecture

Recognition → Orientation → Context → Action → Relational close

1. Recognition — thank, congratulate, acknowledge, validate, or notice the person or effort.
2. Orientation — why this matters or what the sender is willing to do.
3. Context — only what improves trust or understanding.
4. Action — specific and easy next step.
5. Relational close — encouraging, appreciative, confident, supportive, or accountable as the relationship requires.

Not every message needs all five parts at equal length. First-contact email often needs all five. A peer chat ping may be action plus a short close. A delay reply often needs recognition, a short specific context line, then action.

## Context line standard

Generic: "Sorry for the delayed response."

Better when true and short: "I flagged your message yesterday so I wouldn't lose track of it, and then the day got away from me."

That line shows the message was seen, the person mattered, delay was not dismissal, and the sender owns it. Stop there. Do not add a paragraph the recipient now has to soothe.
